# WHO child growth guidance (general reference, not PoshanEye model logic)

WHO Child Growth Standards describe growth from birth to 5 years (0–60 months). WHO's 2007 growth reference is for ages 5–19 years and complements the under-five standards. Choose the appropriate sex- and age-specific indicator/reference and measure accurately. A z-score describes distance from the reference median in standard deviation units: zero is the median, negative is below it, and positive above it.

For children under 5, the commonly used WHO indicators include:

- Height/length-for-age: low values indicate stunting; below -2 SD is stunting and below -3 SD severe stunting.
- Weight-for-age: below -2 SD indicates underweight and below -3 SD severe underweight. This is a composite indicator and does not distinguish short stature from low weight-for-height by itself.
- Weight-for-length/height: below -2 SD indicates wasting and below -3 SD severe wasting.
- BMI-for-age is another age- and sex-specific indicator; BMI must not be interpreted with adult cutoffs in children.

For ages 5–19, WHO reference indicators include height-for-age and BMI-for-age; weight-for-age is available only up to age 10 in the reference. WHO AnthroPlus applies the 5–19 reference and includes continuity with standards for the applicable under-five indicators. These indicators are different and should not be conflated. A trained health professional should interpret measurements in context, including measurement technique, age, sex, illness and growth history.

PoshanEye's current classifier does not calculate WHO z-scores or apply the above thresholds. Its four class names are training labels, not a WHO assessment. Do not state that PoshanEye is WHO-endorsed or clinically validated.
